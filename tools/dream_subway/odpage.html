Transit proposal · fitted to 1,320 point-to-point rides, 2021–2026

Dream Subway

Five lines fitted to where the rides go — not to the streets they take getting there. Stations are the places rides begin and end. Lines are whichever paths through those places carry the most trips without a change. No alignment follows a bike route, because a tunnel has no reason to.

The network

A schematic, not a map: stations sit at their true positions rotated so Manhattan stands upright, but every route between them is drawn as a straight run or a 45° turn. The grey chords behind the lines are the demand itself — one for each pair of places with two or more rides between them. The network is the attempt to cover them.

Line guide

local and express patterns

A stop runs express if it is an interchange or a place with 45 or more recorded arrivals. The bar beside each stop is how many rides have begun or ended there — which on this network is the whole reason the stop exists.

What it was fitted to, and what it misses

The demand is a star, not a grid. 1,320 point-to-point rides spread over 697 distinct pairs of places, and the five heaviest pairs carry only 15% of them — so the network is judged on the long tail, not the headline flows.

The 238 unreachable rides are the honest cost of a 36-station network in a city this size: an end more than 800 m from anywhere the rider has repeatedly been. They are spread thin — no single missing place accounts for more than a handful — so no sixth line recovers them. The 133 stranded rides do reach the network but need two changes, and a sixth line buys back only two points of them.

How it was cut

  • Stations, from trip ends only. The first and last fix of every ride, clustered at 300 m, then merged again at 480 m so no two stops crowd each other. A place needs nine recorded arrivals to earn a stop. Names are the nearest Citi Bike dock.
  • Lines, from the demand matrix. Each line is seeded on the heaviest pair not yet served without a change, then grown by inserting whichever station buys the most unserved demand per kilometre of route it adds. A line may not kink more than 62° at a stop, nor wander more than 1.38× its own straight run, nor repeat more than half of a line already laid down.
  • Five, because the sixth is not worth it. Four lines reach 68% of rides within one change; five reach 72%; six reach 74% and cost another 7 km of route. The count is the knee, not a preference.
  • What this version deliberately drops. An earlier cut of this proposal aligned its lines to the streets the bike actually rides, which put a line down the Hudson River Greenway and a loop around Central Park. Both carry enormous bike traffic and almost no trip ends: they are how the rider travels, not where. Scored on walk access at equal station count, street-derived stations lost to trip-end stations at every radius.
  • What it still cannot say. A trip end is where a recording started, which is not always where the rider did. Frequency, capacity and interchange time are not modelled — “one change” counts a change, not what it costs. And the whole thing is fitted to one person's five years, so it is a portrait, not a plan.
